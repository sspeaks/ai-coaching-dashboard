### Task walkthrough

1. Upload one audio recording and understand that it worked → ✅ Open Upload, choose a file, click Upload recording. During upload the percent/progress bar is clear. Afterward the Feedback page shows the selected recording and a working/ready state.
2. Know what the system is doing while it takes a long time → ⚠️ The progress percent and “This usually takes a few minutes” are helpful, but the page still uses “transcription in progress” and “source recording” instead of consistently user-language status.
3. Read the coaching feedback → ⚠️ Notes are present, but the first thing in every note is a review/uncertainty workflow. The useful coaching takeaway is split across many table rows, making the page feel like an audit form instead of readable coaching notes.
4. Jump back to a critical moment in the audio → ❌ Timestamp chips appear, but from pixels alone I cannot tell they control the audio player, and the actual source recording player is far below the notes on phone. A real user may read the timestamp as a citation, not a play/jump control.
5. Recover when something fails → ❌ The error says what failed but not what the singer should do next. “Try checking for transcript updates after the issue is fixed” gives no owner, fix, or retry path.

Any ❌ on a primary task makes the overall verdict 🔴.

### Viewport comparison

- **Phone 390 and 360:** No obvious horizontal scroll or overlap. Most main buttons are large enough. The long upload disclosure and feedback notes create very tall pages. On phone, the audio player/source recording area is effectively detached from the timestamp buttons by a long scroll, which breaks the “jump back” task.
- **Narrow phone 360:** Layout still fits, but the note review cards are cramped. Radio controls and Save buttons are small compared with the primary buttons. The placeholder recording-name text is clipped.
- **Desktop 1440:** The feedback view uses a useful two-column layout, not just a stretched phone layout. Upload is mostly a wide single-column form; readable, but the data-disclosure block becomes a long wall of text. The audio player remains at the bottom after all notes, far from the timestamp chips.
